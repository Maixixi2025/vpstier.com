### H2-6: The 5-Step Pre-Migration Audit (African-Region Edition)
**Actionable synthesis for the buyer who's about to provision an Africa-region VPS:**

1. **Map your end-user geographic distribution.** Use a 7-day production log; compute median client IP → country; rank top-5. If Africa is 50%+, the African-region pick is correct.
2. **Check the data-residency requirements for your jurisdiction.** If you're serving South African users, check whether POPIA applies to your data class (personal information, health, financial). If yes, in-country or in-region hosting is the most defensible posture.
3. **Verify the provider's AS ownership + datacenter footprint.** Use `whois.iana.org` or `rdap.arin.net/registry/autnums/<ASN>` to confirm the provider's actual AS-ownership. A provider that resells capacity from a Tier-1 carrier doesn't have the same data-residency story as one with their own ASN.
4. **Test latency from inside the region before paying.** From a VPS in South Africa, the article's recommended probe is `ping → 1.1.1.1` + `ping → 8.8.8.8` + a `mtr` to your client's ISP. Numbers should be <5 ms to the local anycast DNS and <30 ms to the client's ISP. If they're outside those bounds, the provider is oversubscribed; pick a different one.
5. **Set up cross-region health-checked DNS failover from day one.** Use Cloudflare or DNSimple's load-balancing features to point your domain to a primary in Africa + a secondary in US/EU. Cost is ~$5-10/mo extra, and the resilience upside is enormous. **CTA anchor (placement 3/6):** the cheapest vetted US/EU secondary is a [RackNerd annual KVM plan](https://my.racknerd.com/aff.php?aff=16299) starting at $21.99/yr — pair with your Sive.Host uMsunduzi River ($5.89/mo) as the primary African tier, and you have a $80-100/yr multi-region setup that gives you <60 ms RTT to African users AND <200 ms RTT to US/EU users from a single $25-30 setup cost.
